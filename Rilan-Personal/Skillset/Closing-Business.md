# Skill — Closing Business

**Area:** Sales
**Why it matters:** Every conversation ends in a decision. Closing isn't pressure — it's giving someone permission to say yes to something that's already right for them.

---

## THE CORE CONCEPT

Closing is not the end of the sale. It's the natural conclusion of a conversation where you've already done everything right. If you have to force a close, something broke earlier.

**The equation:** Certainty + Specificity + Silence = Close

---

## WHAT CLOSING ACTUALLY IS

- **Not:** Tricking someone into saying yes
- **Not:** High-pressure tactics or manipulation
- **Is:** Removing friction between the decision and the action
- **Is:** Being so clear on the value that the next step is obvious
- **Is:** Assumptive — you proceed as if the answer is yes until told otherwise

---

## THE FUNDAMENTALS I'M BUILDING

### 1. The Assumptive Close
Don't ask "do you want to move forward?" — ask "does Tuesday or Thursday work better for the first shoot?"
Two yeses, not one yes/no.

### 2. Commitment Stacking
Get 7 small yeses before the big ask. Each yes builds compliance and investment.
Run the sequence: facts they agree with → observations they confirm → the pain they name themselves → the solution they want.

### 3. Silence After the Close
After you ask — stop talking. The next person to speak loses momentum.
Practice sitting in the silence. It's not dead air. It's processing.

### 4. Name the Cost of Inaction
The price of BTA is $2,500–$4,500/mo. The cost of NOT fixing the gap is $4,500–$10,000/mo.
The close becomes a math problem, not a sales problem.

### 5. Handle Objections Before They Surface
Objections are unaddressed questions. The better your diagnosis (Beats 1–3), the fewer objections appear at the close.

---

## THE 5-BEAT CLOSE STRUCTURE (BTA)

| Beat | Purpose | My Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Open the Frame | Get permission. Show you've done homework. | "I've been looking at your business. Two minutes?" |
| 2 — Surface the Pain | Make THEM say the gap out loud. | Questions only. Let them name it. |
| 3 — Amplify the Cost | Name the monthly revenue leak. Slow down. Let it sit. | Say the number. Then go quiet. |
| 4 — Present the Solution | One tier. Gap cost anchored first. | Lead with the math, not the price. |
| 5 — Close and Commit | Assumptive. Choice between two yeses. | "Tuesday or Thursday?" |

## OBJECTIONS I'M DRILLING

| Objection | The Handle |
|---|---|
| "I need to think about it." | "Is it the investment or not seeing the ROI? Those are different." |
| "Too expensive." | "Compared to the $X/month you're leaving on the table?" |
| "I have someone." | "Are they closing the gap we just talked about?" |
| "Need the third owner." | "What would they need to see? Let's address it now." |
| "Send me something." | "What specifically — so I send the right thing, not a deck." |
