# 1–36 AXIS ASSESSMENT
## Full Lead Scoring Rubric — Confirms Fit After Ghost Score
> Run this after a prospect shows initial interest or after a Ghost Score of 4+. Use it to build the pitch and confirm the right tier.

---

## OVERVIEW

The Axis Assessment scores a business across **6 Signal Categories**, each worth **1–6 points**.

**Maximum score: 36**

The score does NOT measure how good their digital presence is.
It measures **how wide the gap is** between what they've built and what their digital presence shows.

**Higher score = wider gap = stronger revenue recovery story = better BTA fit.**

A business with a score of **30+** is a Digital Emergency — elite quality, nearly invisible online.
A business with a score of **10 or below** may not have the real-world quality to justify BTA.

---

## THE SIX CATEGORIES

---

### CATEGORY 1 — SEARCH PRESENCE
**Max: 6 points**
*How findable is this business through Google Search and Google Business Profile?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | Not ranking in top 3 pages for primary keyword + city. GBP is incomplete, missing photos, no posts, categories inaccurate or generic. Essentially invisible to search-driven buyers. |
| **5** | Ranking page 3–5. GBP present but thin — minimal photos, no posts in 3+ months, no Q&A, generic categories. |
| **4** | Ranking page 2–3. GBP mostly complete but no active posting, few photos, Q&A empty. |
| **3** | Ranking bottom of page 1 or top of page 2. GBP has basics but isn't optimized — no keyword use in description, photos outdated. |
| **2** | Ranking middle of page 1. GBP is active but not dominant — inconsistent posting, decent photos. |
| **1** | Ranking top 3 for primary keyword. GBP is complete, active, well-optimized. Minimal gap here. |

**What to look for:**
- Google `[service] [city]` — what page does this business appear on?
- Open their GBP panel: photo count, last post date, review response rate, categories listed
- Do they appear in the local 3-pack (map results)? If not, that's significant.

---

### CATEGORY 2 — REVIEW VELOCITY & QUALITY
**Max: 6 points**
*Do their reviews reflect the real quality of their business? Are they generating new ones consistently?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | Exceptional reviews (4.7+ avg, real detail in responses) but very low volume (under 25) OR reviews stopped 6+ months ago. Quality vs. quantity mismatch is stark. |
| **5** | Strong average (4.5+) with moderate volume (25–75) but no recent activity — review velocity has stalled. No response to reviews. |
| **4** | Good reviews but mixed consistency — some 5-star, some 3-star, no clear trend. No review generation system visible. |
| **3** | Decent volume (50–150) but average is dragged down (3.8–4.3) by unaddressed negative reviews. Responses sporadic. |
| **2** | Reviews present and relatively current, but average is below 4.5 or volume is low relative to business age. |
| **1** | Strong review presence — high volume, 4.6+ average, active responses, steady velocity. Minimal gap here. |

**What to look for:**
- Total Google review count
- Average rating (to one decimal)
- Date of most recent review
- Date of last owner response
- Review content: are they specific (mentioning the owner, a transformation, a specific experience) or generic?
- Are negative reviews addressed or ignored?

---

### CATEGORY 3 — SOCIAL MEDIA SIGNAL
**Max: 6 points**
*Is their social presence building trust and creating signal, or is it a ghost town?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | No active social presence OR last post 4+ months ago. Platform exists but is effectively abandoned. No video content whatsoever. |
| **5** | Sporadic activity — posts every 2–4 weeks, inconsistent quality. Follower count doesn't reflect business quality. No reels/video. Mix of personal and business content. |
| **4** | Some consistency (weekly posts) but content doesn't reflect the craft — no before/after, no client results, no authority content. Pure promotional. |
| **3** | Regular posting but engagement is flat. No video. Followers don't reflect true reach. Content is decent but doesn't convert or demonstrate expertise. |
| **2** | Active and consistent. Some video. Decent engagement. But one platform only and content isn't fully leveraging what they've built. |
| **1** | Active on 2+ platforms. Strong video presence. Content showcases craft and results. Real follower growth and engagement. Minimal gap here. |

**What to look for:**
- Instagram: follower count, last post date, posts in last 90 days (count), reel count, engagement (likes + comments ÷ followers × 100 = engagement rate — 2%+ is healthy)
- Facebook: activity level, review/recommendation count
- YouTube: channel existence, video count, subscriber count
- Is the content showing their actual work? Or is it generic?
- Does the visual quality match the quality of their real work?

---

### CATEGORY 4 — WEBSITE QUALITY
**Max: 6 points**
*Does the website represent the business accurately and convert visitors into inquiries?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | No website OR website is clearly outdated (pre-2019 design, broken links, no mobile optimization). Does not represent the business at all. |
| **5** | Website exists but is low-quality — template-style, minimal content, no photos of actual work or team, no testimonials, no clear CTAs. Might as well not exist. |
| **4** | Functional website but no conversion elements — no calls to action, contact form buried, no testimonials or social proof, no service descriptions. |
| **3** | Decent website that needs updating — content is accurate but photos are old, copy is generic, no real differentiation or story. |
| **2** | Good website with most elements present — clear CTAs, some testimonials, mobile-friendly — but not optimized and doesn't capture their full story. |
| **1** | Strong website — converts visitors, reflects brand, mobile-optimized, has testimonials, case studies, clear CTAs, and genuine personality. Minimal gap. |

**What to look for:**
- Open on mobile — is it responsive?
- Is there a clear CTA above the fold?
- Can you find real photos of their work (not stock)?
- Are there testimonials with names and details?
- Is the copy specific or generic ("trusted professionals serving San Antonio" vs. "licensed master electricians with 18 years specializing in custom home electrical")?
- When was it last updated (footer copyright year is a clue)?

---

### CATEGORY 5 — AUTHORITY CONTENT
**Max: 6 points**
*Do they have content that demonstrates expertise and builds trust before a buyer ever contacts them?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | No authority content whatsoever — no case studies, no transformation content, no video, no thought leadership, no before/after. Their expertise is invisible. |
| **5** | Minimal authority content — one or two testimonials on website, no video, no case studies, no real documentation of their craft or results. |
| **4** | Some proof elements — a few testimonials, some before/after photos, but not formatted as authority content. No story, no context. |
| **3** | Decent proof presence — multiple testimonials, some before/after content — but unorganized and not compelling. No video case studies. |
| **2** | Good authority foundation — multiple strong testimonials, some video, clear documentation of results — but not fully developed. |
| **1** | Strong authority content — case studies, transformation video, thought leadership, multiple platforms of proof. Buyers are pre-sold before calling. Minimal gap. |

**What to look for:**
- Before/after documentation (photos, video)
- Named, specific testimonials (not anonymous or generic)
- Case studies or client outcome stories
- YouTube tutorials, interviews, or expertise demonstrations
- Podcast appearances, press mentions, awards
- Content that would make a buyer say "this person clearly knows what they're doing"

---

### CATEGORY 6 — LEAD INFRASTRUCTURE
**Max: 6 points**
*Is the business set up to capture and convert the buyers their signal should be generating?*

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **6** | No digital lead capture whatsoever — no online booking, no contact form that works, no clear path for a new buyer to take action. Buying is difficult. |
| **5** | Minimal infrastructure — a phone number somewhere, a basic contact form — but no clear CTA, no booking system, no follow-up mechanism. |
| **4** | Some infrastructure in place but inconsistent — CTA exists but isn't prominent, booking link buried, phone number not clickable on mobile. |
| **3** | Functional lead capture — contact form works, CTA is present — but not optimized. No tracking, no offer, no incentive to act now. |
| **2** | Good infrastructure — online booking, clear CTA, mobile-friendly contact — but no lead magnet, no urgency element, not maximized. |
| **1** | Optimized lead infrastructure — booking system prominent, multiple CTAs, mobile-optimized, possibly with lead magnet or offer. Minimal gap. |

**What to look for:**
- Is there an online booking option?
- Is the phone number clickable on mobile?
- Can you find the contact form in under 10 seconds?
- Is there a clear next step ("Book a Consultation," "Get Your Free Estimate," "Reserve Your Spot")?
- Is there any follow-up mechanism (email capture, newsletter, SMS opt-in)?

## TOTAL SCORE + INTERPRETATION

### Add all six category scores.

| Total Score | Classification | Recommended Tier | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **30–36** | **Digital Emergency** | Signal Prime or Ops | Maximum gap. Elite quality, near-invisible presence. This is the highest-priority Ghost. Revenue recovery story writes itself. |
| **22–29** | **High-Value Ghost** | Signal Prime | Significant gap, strong quality. Clear revenue recovery story. Ready for full-system build. |
| **14–21** | **Active Haunt** | Signal Entry | Meaningful gap. Quality is real but not elite. Good starting point, build toward Prime. |
| **8–13** | **Developing Signal** | Signal Entry | Gap exists but isn't dramatic. May need some quality development alongside signal work. Confirm quality signals before pitching. |
| **1–7** | **Not a BTA Fit** | None — move on | Their presence is already solid OR their real-world quality doesn't justify the investment. Don't pitch. |

## HOW TO USE THE AXIS ASSESSMENT IN A CALL

You don't show the prospect the rubric. You USE the rubric to build the pitch.

**In the pitch:**
> *"I ran a full assessment on your digital presence — six areas, and here's where you stand: [Top 2 category gaps]. Your biggest opportunity is [Category X]. That's where the revenue leak is widest. That's also where we start."*

**Use specific scores to anchor the severity:**
> *"On search presence, you're scoring near the bottom — you're effectively invisible for '[keyword] San Antonio.' That's not a guess — I checked. Anyone searching for what you do in this city isn't finding you."*

**Translate to dollars:**
> *"Across the six areas, your gap is telling me somewhere between [$X and $Y] in monthly revenue that's not reaching you. That's what Signal [Entry/Prime] closes."*
