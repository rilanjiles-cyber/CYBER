# BTA SALES SYSTEM
## The Mentality · Built on Grant Cardone + Andy Elliott
> You are not selling marketing. You are closing a revenue leak.

## THE MINDSET

### You Are the Rescue

Every business you call has a leak. Money is walking out the door every single day because buyers can't find them online. That's not a hypothesis — that's a fact you confirmed before you picked up the phone.

You are not interrupting their day to pitch a service.

You are arriving to show them exactly where the blood is coming from — and offering to stop it.

**That's not a pitch. That's a rescue.**

### Cardone Principles — Non-Negotiable

**1. 10X the Activity**
Whatever you think the minimum is, multiply it by 10. You should be reaching out to more businesses than feels comfortable. The market is massive. The Ghosts are everywhere. Volume is how you find the ones ready to move.

**2. Speed to Lead**
When someone responds — to a DM, an email, a text — you are on the phone within 5 minutes. Not an hour. Not tomorrow. Five minutes. Leads go cold in real time. The operator who gets there first wins.

**3. Follow Up or Die**
The fortune is in the follow-up. 80% of sales happen after the 5th contact. Most operators quit after one. Three-touch minimum. Every prospect. No exceptions.

**4. "Not Interested" is Not a No**
"Not interested" means *not interested yet.* It means you haven't shown them the cost of their gap clearly enough. It means the frame wasn't right. Come back in 2 weeks with new data. "Not interested" is a touchpoint, not a close.

**5. Money is Not the Objection**
If a prospect says the price is too high, they don't believe the value yet. The price is never the problem — the ROI isn't clear enough. Go back to the gap. Make the cost of NOT fixing it bigger than the cost of fixing it.

## THE 5-BEAT CALL STRUCTURE

Every BTA call has five beats. Learn them cold. Do them in order.

---

### Beat 1 — Open the Frame
**Goal:** Get permission for 2 minutes. Establish you've done your homework.

**Script:**
> *"Hey [Name], this is [Rilan] with Blitz The Axis. I've been looking at your business — the reviews, the presence, what I can find online. Got about two minutes?"*

**Rules:**
- Do not pitch in Beat 1. Not one word of pitch.
- State your name and company. Full stop.
- "I've been looking at your business" is the hook. It signals you're not cold-calling.
- If they say no or not now — ask for a specific time. Get the appointment.
- If they say yes — pause one second, then go to Beat 2.

**If they ask "what is this about?":**
> *"I found something in your online presence I want to show you — it's quick. Two minutes?"*

---

### Beat 2 — Surface the Pain
**Goal:** Make THEM name the gap. Don't describe it — ask them to describe it.

**Script framework:**
> *"I pulled your Google listing, went through your reviews — you've got [X] reviews, [average rating]. When someone searches '[their service] in [city]' right now, what page does your business show up on?"*

Let them answer.

> *"And is that where you feel like you should be, based on what you've built?"*

Let them answer.

**Rules:**
- Questions only in Beat 2. No statements.
- The goal is to hear them say — in their own words — that the gap is real.
- If they don't know the answer (what page they rank on), that IS the answer.
- Don't rush past this. The pain needs to breathe.
- If they say "yeah we don't really have much going on online" — that's your signal to go to Beat 3.

**Alternative opener if they're warm:**
> *"You've got [X] five-star reviews — that's real. But I couldn't find you on page one for [primary keyword]. Is that by design, or is that something you've been meaning to address?"*

---

### Beat 3 — Amplify the Cost
**Goal:** Put a number on the gap. Make the leak real. Slow down and let it sit.

**Script:**
> *"Here's what I found. You're averaging [X] clients/customers a month. Your ticket average — based on what I can see — is somewhere around [estimated range]. You're ranking on page [X] for '[keyword].' Page one gets 70% of the clicks. Page 3 gets less than 5%. Every month, [Y number] people are searching for exactly what you do in [city], finding your competitor first, and never finding you. That's not a marketing problem. That's a revenue problem."*

Pause. Let that sit for 3 seconds.

> *"Based on what you've told me — and what I can see — you're looking at [estimated range $X–$Y] in monthly revenue that could be yours. That's the Axis Gap. That's what's bleeding."*

**Rules:**
- Name a real number. Don't be vague.
- Slow down when you say the dollar figure.
- After you say it — go silent. Let them sit with it.
- Do not jump to the solution yet. Make the cost land.
- You don't need to be exact. An educated estimate is powerful. "Somewhere between $3,000 and $6,000 a month" is more impactful than "some amount."

**How to estimate the monthly leak:**
```
Monthly searches for [keyword] in [city] × 0.28 (page 1 click rate)
= Estimated monthly searchers who COULD find them
× Conversion rate (estimate 5–10% for service businesses)
= Monthly leads available
× Average ticket price
= Monthly revenue sitting on the table
```

---

### Beat 4 — Present the Solution
**Goal:** Present one tier, anchored to the gap cost.

**Rules:**
- One tier. Not three options. Not a menu. One recommendation.
- Always anchor to the gap cost FIRST, then present the price.
- Never lead with price.

**Script:**
> *"Based on what I'm seeing — the gap, where you're at, what you need to close — I'd put you at Signal [Entry/Prime]. Here's what that looks like and what it closes..."*

[Walk through what the tier covers — max 90 seconds.]

> *"And the investment is [price]/month. Compare that to [gap cost] you're leaving on the table every month — this is a math problem, not a budget question."*

**Tier selection guide:**
| Scenario | Recommend |
|---|---|
| Gap is clear, early stage, needs foundation | Signal Entry $2,500 |
| Gap is wide, business is ready to move fast | Signal Prime $4,500 |
| Multi-location, complex, enterprise | Signal Ops — custom discovery call |

---

### Beat 5 — Close and Commit
**Goal:** Assumptive close. Choice between two yeses.

**The Assumptive Close:**
> *"Based on everything we've talked about — let's do this. I'll get the onboarding doc over to you today. Do you want to start with [date] or is [one week later] better for you?"*

Do not ask "Do you want to move forward?" That's a yes/no question. Give them two dates.

**The Choice Close:**
> *"Which works better for you — Signal Entry to get the foundation right, or Signal Prime to go all the way?"*

**Rules:**
- After you close — go silent.
- The next person who speaks loses momentum.
- Do not add value after the close. Do not keep selling.
- If they go quiet — let them think. Silence is not dead air. It's processing.

## COMMITMENT STACKING

Before you ask for the big yes, stack 7 small yeses. Each one increases compliance and investment in the conversation.

**The 7-Step Yes Sequence:**

1. *"You've been in business for [X] years, right?"* — Yes
2. *"And you've built this through word of mouth and repeat clients?"* — Yes
3. *"So the quality of what you do is real — you know that."* — Yes
4. *"The issue isn't the work. The issue is what happens when someone who doesn't know you searches for you online."* — Yes
5. *"And right now, when they search, they're finding [competitor] first — not you."* — Yes (or silence — still a yes)
6. *"So every month, there are buyers who would have chosen you — if they could have found you."* — Yes
7. *"That's revenue you've already earned that just isn't getting to you."* — Yes

Now close.

## FULL OBJECTION MATRIX

### "I need to think about it."

This is the most common soft stall. They're not saying no — they're saying they're not sure yet.

**Handle:**
> *"I get it — this isn't a small decision. Help me understand: is it the investment, or is it not seeing how it translates to revenue for your business?"*

[Wait for the real objection. Handle that one.]

If they say investment:
> *"What if I could show you that the cost of not fixing this is higher than the cost of fixing it? Because that math is already there."*

If they say ROI:
> *"Fair. Let me show you what this looked like for [similar client]. [Result]. That's what closing the gap looks like. And that business was in a similar spot to yours."*

---

### "It's too expensive."

This is a value objection disguised as a price objection.

**Handle:**
> *"I hear you. Tell me — what would make the $[X]/month feel like an easy yes for you?"*

[Let them answer.]

Then:
> *"So if we could show you [X result] inside 90 days, the investment makes sense. That's exactly what we're built to do. And the alternative is you keep leaving [$Y/month] on the table. That math doesn't get better with time."*

---

### "I already have someone handling that."

**Handle:**
> *"Good — having someone is better than having no one. Let me ask: when's the last time you ranked on page one for [primary keyword] in [city]? And what's your review velocity — how many new reviews are you getting each month?"*

[Let them answer.]

> *"I ask because what I'm seeing says there's still a gap between what you've built and what the internet shows. If your current person is handling it, that gap should be closing. Is it?"*

---

### "The timing isn't right."

**Handle:**
> *"I hear that. The thing is — the gap isn't waiting. Every month you're not on page one, buyers are going somewhere else. When would the timing be right?"*

[Get a date.]

> *"Let me mark that. In the meantime, can I send you something so you can see exactly what I'm looking at? No commitment — just the data."*

[Get permission. Send the brief. Follow up on the date they gave.]

---

### "Send me something first."

**Handle:**
> *"Absolutely. And I want to make sure I send you the right thing — not a generic deck. Two questions first: [question about their gap], [question about their goal]. That way what I send is specific to your situation."*

[Get the answers. Build a tailored brief. Use it as Touch 2.]

---

### "I need to ask my partner/spouse."

**Handle:**
> *"Makes sense — big decisions should be made together. Help me set this up right: when do you two usually talk through business decisions like this?"*

[Get a time.]

> *"What if we did a 15-minute call — all three of us — so they can ask any questions directly? That way you're not playing telephone with the details."*

## PHONE RULES

1. **Smile before you dial.** It changes your tone. Scientifically proven.
2. **Stand up.** Energy transfers through voice. Standing gives you more of it.
3. **Never apologize for calling.** "Sorry to bother you" kills your frame in 4 words.
4. **Use their name once — early.** After that, sparingly. Over-use kills rapport.
5. **Match their pace — then lead.** If they're slow, meet them there. Then gradually accelerate.
6. **Don't fill silence.** Let pauses breathe. Silence is not your enemy.
7. **End with a commitment, not a maybe.** No call ends without a clear next step.
8. **Write the outcome immediately.** Log in HubSpot before the next call.

## ANDY ELLIOTT'S 5 TONALITY PRINCIPLES

### 1. Certainty
You must believe — completely — that closing the Axis Gap will change their business. If you don't believe it, they won't either. Certainty is contagious. So is doubt.

### 2. Urgency
The gap is costing them money right now. Today. Not eventually. The urgency is real — communicate it that way. "Every month this isn't closed is another month of lost revenue" is not pressure. It's math.

### 3. Empathy
Understand that most business owners have been burned by marketers making big promises. Lead with what you know — their specific situation, their specific gap. Show them you did the work.

### 4. Confidence ≠ Aggression
Confidence says "I know this is the right move for your business." Aggression says "you're an idiot if you don't sign." One closes deals. One kills them. Know the difference.

### 5. Silence is a Weapon
After the close — stop talking. The next person who speaks has a psychological disadvantage. Silence is not discomfort. It's power. Practice sitting in it.
